Noninvasive methods for continuous wearable biomarker detection research in the infrared spectrum remain conceptual due to the size and power consumption of infrared sensors. This paper introduces IR2, a continuous noninvasive wearable device that uses multispectral optical sensing to enable multi-biomarker research. We characterized IR2 through benchtop evaluations of device variability, environmental robustness, and power performance, then deployed it in exercise, clinical, nutrition, and eating studies totaling more than 560 hours of optical data from 79 participants. These deployments revealed design limitations and informed improvements to communication reliability, analog signal conditioning, battery life, and enclosure design.
