This project addresses inequities in pulse oximetry by treating skin pigmentation as a measured design variable rather than an unmeasured source of error. The work will connect controlled physical standards with prospective human measurements and prepare an open equity-validation package for later clinical testing.

### Research Focus
- Develop and characterize reusable static skin-tone optical phantoms spanning controlled pigmentation-like absorption and tissue-relevant scattering
- Prospectively validate objective skin-tone sensing in the integrated Lumos + SpO2 platform across a diverse adult cohort
- Establish phantom-to-human correspondence, transparent coverage metrics, and reproducible calibration procedures
- Release open specifications, calibration records, data dictionaries, and analysis tools to support broader and more equitable device validation
